Crowned A4 Landscape 2×4 v1

Print the CLC PDF on 210 × 297 mm A4 at Actual Size. Use the Crowned A4
Landscape 2×4 preset. The PDF is portrait as it feeds, with the cards
turned sideways. Load the short edge marked FEED THIS EDGE FIRST.

The DXF contains the eight rounded cut paths. Open it in Silhouette
Studio and keep the paths selected and grouped. Use a 12 × 12 in mat,
millimeter units, and a custom portrait Studio page of 218 × 301 mm.
This page belongs to Studio. The print PDF uses physical A4.

If Studio changes the DXF scale or centers it when importing, normalize
the full group in the Transform panel. Set width to 177.207 mm and
height to 255.778 mm. Place its top left at X 20.367 mm, Y 16.023 mm
relative to the page's top-left corner. These dimensions are for the
whole group of eight paths. Leave all eight in place while sizing.

Enable the default three-mark pattern with a square in the upper left.
In Studio with a Cameo 5 Alpha, select the Cameo 5 layout for three marks.
Set mark length to 5 mm and thickness to 1 mm. Under Advanced Options,
set the top, left and right insets to 10 mm and the bottom inset to
23 mm. Check that the cut paths and all printed art are clear of the
registration areas.

On the physical mat, position the A4 sheet 4 mm right and 4 mm down
from the mat grid's top-left origin, with the square mark at the upper
left. This offset aligns the A4 sheet to the larger virtual Studio page.
The bottom edge is 301 mm from the top of the mat grid.

Before cards, verify registration and all eight cut positions with a
plain A4 proof at Actual Size. It should also measure about 88 × 63 mm
per cut in the orientation you see on the mat. Save the setup in
Studio as a .studio3 after checking it. For partial sheets, keep the
same placement and send cut lines only for the slots containing cards.

Check print quality with Crowned stock separately. For backs, allow the
fronts to dry, turn the sheet over along its long edge and keep the
same marked short edge entering first. Check alignment on both faces
before releasing a larger batch.
